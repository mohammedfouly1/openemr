## 1. Why one call, not three

The requirements document is explicit that this is a **single combined interview**, not three separate
research tracks:

> RDY-0076: *"Same 10 respondents plus 3 clinic accountants"*
> RDY-0077: *"Same 10 respondents"*

All three validations draw on the same 10 clinic-owner/manager interviews (V-2 adds 3 accountant-only
calls on top, for its own respondent pool). Ten calls, run once each, close three RDYs' worth of
evidence — provided the **order inside the call** is right, because getting it wrong invalidates V-3
specifically (see §2).

## 2. Call sequencing — why this order, not another

| Order | Test | Card constraint |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Consent / screening | No product content — must precede everything test-related |
| 2 | **V-1** payer mix | RDY-0075: *"opening with payer mix before anything else is discussed"* |
| 3 | **V-3** open pain question | RDY-0077: *"an open question asked before any pitch"* |
| 4 | **V-2** boundary question | Explicit "clinical system separate from invoicing" pitch |
| 5 | Close | — |

**V-3 must run before V-2, not after**, even though both are "in the same call." V-2's script
necessarily describes the product boundary (clinical system vs. invoicing) — the first point in the
call where the interviewer introduces product-shaped language. If V-3's open question ran after that,
any access/control mention from the respondent would already be contaminated by what the interviewer
just said, and RDY-0077's core requirement — **unprompted** — could never be satisfied honestly. Running
V-3 first, before any pitch of any kind, is the only order that keeps V-3's evidence real.

V-1's payer-mix question is fully neutral (asks for a ratio, introduces no product concept), so it can
safely open the call without contaminating V-3.

## 4. MAIN INTERVIEW GUIDE — clinic owners / managers (10 calls, ~35–45 min each)

Read scripted lines **as written**. Do not paraphrase the boundary language in Q3 or the open question
in Q2 — wording changes are exactly what turns a neutral instrument into a leading one.

### 4.1 Opening / consent (not scored)

> "Hi, thank you for taking the time to speak with me. I'm doing some short research calls with clinic
> owners and managers in Saudi Arabia to understand how clinics like yours handle patients and billing
> today. This isn't a sales call — I'm not going to describe any product for the first part of our
> conversation. It should take about 35 to 45 minutes. Is that alright? Before we start, is it okay if
> I take notes on this call for internal review only?"

### 4.2 Screening (not scored, but required for the mix check in §3)

- Clinic name: __________
- City: ☐ Riyadh ☐ Jeddah ☐ Other: __________
- Respondent role: ☐ Owner ☐ Manager ☐ Physician-owner ☐ Other: __________
- Clinic specialty: __________
- Approx. number of providers: __________

### 4.3 Q1 — payer mix (scores V-1 / RDY-0075) — MUST be the first substantive question

> **"Roughly speaking, out of every 10 patients you see, how many pay out of pocket themselves, and how
> many are billed through an insurance company or a payer contract?"**

**Interviewer rules:**
- Do not use the words "self-pay" or "majority." Do not hint at an expected split.
- If the answer is vague ("mostly," "a lot"), the only permitted follow-up is: *"Can you put a rough
  number or percentage on that?"* Do not suggest a number yourself.
- Accept a range if that's what's given; record the range verbatim, do not force a single figure.

**Record:** verbatim response · reported split · classification (Majority self-pay: **Yes / No /
Unclear**) — this feeds §8.1.

### 4.4 Q2 — unprompted pain probe (scores V-3 / RDY-0077) — MUST precede any pitch, MUST precede Q3

> **"Thinking about how patient records are handled at your clinic today — from the moment a patient
> arrives to how their history is stored and used later — what goes wrong, or what's frustrating about
> that process?"**

**Permitted neutral follow-ups only** (use as many as useful, in any order, but introduce nothing new):
- *"Can you say more about that?"*
- *"Is there anything else that comes to mind?"*
- *"What would you change about that if you could?"*

**Do not**, during this question or its follow-ups, say any of: access, control, own/ownership, portable
/ portability, traceability, audit, take your data, switch systems, lock-in, or any close paraphrase.
The first time the interviewer (not the respondent) uses any of those words or a clear paraphrase, in
this question or later, is the **PROMPT LINE** for this call — mark it explicitly in the transcript
(§6) with the exact words used and where in the call it happened. Everything the respondent volunteers
**before** the PROMPT LINE, on the access/traceability/ownership theme, is **UNPROMPTED**. Anything
after is **PROMPTED**. This is tracked per mention, not per question, because a respondent may raise the
theme later in the call (e.g., during the Q3 discussion) — if that happens before the interviewer's own
PROMPT LINE, it still counts as unprompted.

**Optional prompted follow-up** — only after two rounds of the neutral probes above produce nothing
further, and only if the interviewer wants a reaction reading for context (this answer is recorded but
**never** counts toward the ≥4-of-10 threshold):

> *"Some clinics have mentioned they worry about who can access their patient records, or about being
> able to take their records with them if they ever changed systems or providers — does anything like
> that come up for you?"*

Using this line sets the PROMPT LINE for the call if it hasn't already been set.

**Record:** verbatim transcript of the whole exchange · mention log (§6.3) · PROMPT LINE marker (or
"not reached" if the respondent never triggered it and the interviewer never used the optional prompt).

### 4.5 Q3 — explicit boundary question (scores V-2 / RDY-0076) — runs last, after Q2 is fully closed out

> **"I want to describe something and get your honest reaction — including if the honest reaction is
> 'no.' Imagine a system that manages the clinical side only: patient records, visit notes,
> prescriptions, results. It does not replace your invoicing, your ZATCA-compliant billing, or your
> accounting system — those stay exactly as they are today, completely separate. This system would run
> alongside what you already use for billing, not instead of it. Knowing that it does not touch billing
> or invoicing at all, would you be willing to run a system like that alongside what you have now?"**

**Interviewer rules:**
- Read the boundary sentence exactly as written — it is the "boundary drawn explicitly" the card
  requires (RDY-0076, line 8545).
- Do not answer "would it save you time / money" or any other question the respondent didn't ask —
  answer only what's asked, then return to listening.

**If hesitation or "no," the only permitted follow-up:**
> *"What's the hesitation?"* / *"What would make that a 'no' for you?"*

**Record:** verbatim response · classification (see §8.2): **Yes-without-hesitation / Yes-with-hesitation
(quote the hesitation) / No / "Then what's the point?" or equivalent objection (quote it)**.

### 4.6 Closing (not scored)

> "Thank you so much for your time and for being so candid — this is genuinely useful. No commitment on
> your part, and I won't follow up unless you'd like me to."

Log actual call duration.
